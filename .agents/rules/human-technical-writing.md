# Human-Crafted Technical Writing Standards

1. Tone and Perspective:
   - Write as an experienced technical communicator or product specialist guiding a real clinician or researcher.
   - Explain the context and medical rationale behind features, not merely their mechanical buttons.
   - Strictly avoid artificial AI buzzwords and tropes ("In today's digital era...", "It is important to note that...", "Furthermore, it is worth mentioning...", "This comprehensive platform provides...").

2. Structural and Typographic Discipline:
   - Prefer complete, well-reasoned narrative paragraphs over bullet listicles.
   - Never bold words haphazardly inside sentences. Use bolding strictly for headings and initial term definitions.
   - Avoid unconstrained, repetitive bullet points.

3. Healthcare and Community Governance (PatientsLikeMe Reference Model):
   - Uphold patient privacy: require complete removal of patient identifiers (de-identification) on diagnostic images (MRI, CT, EEG) and clinical discussions.
   - Frame knowledge contributions around real-world clinical evidence, continuing medical education (CME), and professional development.
   - Maintain strict separation between medical education and formal medical advice.

4. Plain Text Formatting:
   - When generating `.txt` manuals, format strictly for plain text editors (Notepad, nano, vim): UTF-8 encoding, uppercase chapter headers, ASCII underline bars, 4-space paragraph indentations, and zero Markdown rendering syntax.
